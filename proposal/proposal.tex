\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries EN.601.762 Project Proposal}\\[0.35em]
{\bfseries Verified Doom Level Generation: A Playability-Checked Intermediate Representation for Language Agents}\\[0.25em]
{\small Fall 2026 \quad$\cdot$\quad Due Sep~16}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Team members:} Xuyang Wang, Shreyas Phegade\\

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Language models can describe a good Doom level, but the maps they produce often cannot be finished: rooms overlap, props block doorways, lifts strand the player, and keys sit behind their own locks.
Our pilot maps scored well on path-clarity heuristics yet failed fair play in GZDoom because of exactly these geometry errors.
We argue the bottleneck is not the prompt but the absence of a \emph{checkable contract} between the model and the engine.
We contribute (1)~an intermediate representation (IR) that encodes a level as a \emph{critical-path graph} (start, teach, key, lock, peak, exit, with precedence and optional branches) plus typed geometry (true L-shaped rooms, doors, lifts, cover props, monster closets);
(2)~a deterministic compiler from IR to UDMF that is \emph{verified}: before writing a PWAD it runs a hull and walkability gate (room and closet overlap, hollow or portal-blocking props, incomplete door and lift openings, and a breadth-first search at Doom's 16-unit player radius from spawn to exit) and refuses to emit failing maps;
(3)~a layered metric suite that separates \emph{playable} (binary, from the gate), \emph{trajectory} (key-before-lock, weapon-before-peak, early-combat cap, hub return, landmark clearance), and \emph{design} (a five-pillar rubric), so that a pretty but broken level scores zero;
and (4)~a verifier-guided repair loop in which gate and trajectory failures are returned to the generator as structured, localized error messages instead of free-form critique.
The generator itself is interchangeable: we evaluate local Ollama models and a rule-based baseline under the same IR, compiler, and metrics, so gains can be attributed to the representation and verifier rather than to prompting a particular model.
Inference runs locally (RTX~5070, 16\,GB) with free assets (Freedoom).

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Grounding.}
The IR follows Riedl's view that in games, story is progression through space: experience management and GAME FORGE encode intended play as a plot graph of ordered events with optional branches, realized as ``islands'' (required rooms) joined by ``bridges'' (corridors), and we enforce that optional wings never sit on the only route between plot nodes.
The repair loop follows Rockt\"aschel's framing of open-ended systems as producing novel but learnable artifacts through cheap, strict environments and self-improvement loops: our gate is the learnability filter, structured failures are the improvement signal, and a stretch goal is a quality--diversity archive of level patterns that keeps only maps passing the gate.

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{Research questions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} Does the critical-path IR raise the rate of finishable maps over generating raw room/connection JSON with the same model?
  \item \textbf{RQ2:} Does structured verifier feedback repair more failures per iteration than free-form LLM or VLM critique?
  \item \textbf{RQ3:} Do the automatic playable and trajectory metrics agree with human fair-play outcomes better than design heuristics alone?
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{Evaluation.}
Primary metric: \emph{verified-finishable rate} (passes the gate and the trajectory checks), confirmed on a subset by human fair play in GZDoom without cheats.
Secondary: repairs needed to reach a pass, trajectory and design scores, and diversity across briefs.
Baselines: rule-based generator; LLM emitting flat JSON without the IR; IR without the gate; IR with gate but free-form critique.
Ablations remove one component at a time (IR, gate, structured feedback, classic-map few-shot references).

\noindent\textbf{Planned mid deliverables (Oct~19)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item IR schema and validator; UDMF compiler with L-rooms, doors, keyed locks, lifts, props, and closets.
  \item Hull and walkability gate integrated as a hard build step, with unit tests on known failure cases from our playtests.
  \item Layered metrics (playable / trajectory / design) on at least 10 generated maps, with a failure gallery that labels which check caught each bug.
  \item End-to-end demo: brief $\rightarrow$ IR $\rightarrow$ verified \texttt{.wad} in UDB and GZDoom.
  \item Mid report (NeurIPS 2026 format, $\leq$4 pages) and in-class presentation.
